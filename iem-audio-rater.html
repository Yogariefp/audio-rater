💡 How to notice: Focus on hi-hats and cymbals. Can you hear the metallic shimmer and the decay tail after each hit? Also listen for subtle background details — a tambourine, a breath before a vocal phrase, or a distant backing instrument. If you notice new things each listen, detail is high.

2Very dull; hi-hats are barely audible shapes
3Dull; hi-hats are vague blobs with no shimmer
4Below average; treble is muted and lacks definition
5Average; treble present but limited in resolution
6Decent; cymbal shimmer partially audible
7Detailed; shimmer, decay, and micro-details audible
8Very detailed; subtle background elements emerge
9Excellent; you notice new micro-details each listen
10Hyper-detailed; every transient and harmonic resolved
Sibilance / Harshness
Painful "S" and "T" sounds, shrill peaks
6
None 👍MildHarsh 👎
💡 How to notice: Listen to the singer say words with "s", "sh", or "ts" sounds (e.g. "still", "she", "this"). If those sounds sting your ears or feel like a sharp spike, that's sibilance. Cymbal crashes that sound razor-sharp or piercing also indicate treble harshness. Lower score = better here.
📊 Score guide ▼
1No sibilance at all; perfectly smooth treble ✅ (best)
2Imperceptible; virtually no harshness detectable
3Very slight; barely noticeable on "s" sounds
4Slight; only apparent on very sibilant recordings
5Mild; occasional sharpness on sibilant tracks
6Moderate; "s" sounds are noticeably edgy
7Noticeable; "s" and "t" words regularly sting
8Strong; sibilance is a consistent annoyance
9Severe; listening is uncomfortable on many tracks
10Painful; ear fatigue within minutes ❌ (worst)

6Decent; most instruments stay mostly distinct
7Good; each instrument has its own clear space
8Very good; separation holds even in complex mixes
9Excellent; effortlessly separates all elements
10Perfect; isolate any single instrument at will

7Good; you can point to where each instrument is
8Very good; positions are stable and convincing

💡 Test Tips

🎵 Sub-bass: Play "Midnight City" by M83 or any electronic with deep synths.


🥁 Punch: Use "Swashers" by Yosi Hirokawa or metal/rock with fast kick drums.


🎤 Vocals: Use "Sleepwalking Cover" by This Wildlife or an acoustic singer-songwriter track you know well.


🔔 Sibilance: Tracks with lots of "s" words — if they sting, it's sibilant.


🌐 Soundstage: Use a binaural recording (search "binaural 8D audio" on YouTube).


🎯 Imaging: Orchestral music — can you tell where violin vs cello sits?
